# Fire Dance

*James Tate*

In your eyes, I am a wealth of sin

You see me with kegs of cognac,

harems of nubile lovers

bathing me in sweet-smelling oil.

You see me smoking opium

in the early hours, drowsy,

<br>

nibbling at a quail's wing

and a naked girl. O once in a while

I roll over and dictate a poem:

I smile graciously at this 

charming act of condescension

and my thirty-three wives applaud

<br>

quietly. The next day it appears

in *The New Yorker* and I donate

the paltry reward to the veterans

of the Turko-Cuban Civil War

or the survivors of the Flemish

fire sale. Yes, Sister Michele,

<br>

it is all true: the fire dance

you are waiting for in the next

life happens every night

on my living room floor:

I write and read and write

until almost dawn until

<br>

there is not a light on

in the world and the ghosts

of beauty have disppeared

and my pride has crossed over

into complete shame and 

emptiness and the thrush says

sleep you goddamned fool.
